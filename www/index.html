About me

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Rochester. I did my Ph.D. at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI), fortunate to be advised by Prof. Tianyi Chen (now at Cornell). I obtained my M.S. in Electrical Engineering from RPI in 2021, and my B.S. from Huazhong University of Science and Technology (HUST) in 2017.

My research lies at the intersection of machine learning, optimization, and statistical signal/image processing, with a current focus on the theory and applications of multi-objective and multi-level optimization and learning. Motivated by real-world scientific and engineering systems, I develop foundations to address problems involving competing objectives, resource constraints, multiple data modalities, heterogeneous users and devices, and hierarchical/multi-criteria decision-making under uncertain environments. I aim to connect theoretical insights with practical needs.

I am always looking for highly motivated students and postdocs. If you are interested, please check out more details here.

Our paper on uniform coverage of the Pareto front is accepted by NeurIPS 2026. Congrats to Liuyuan Jiang and Chentong Huang! We will present this work in Atlanta, GA, this December.
SURF: Steering the Scalarization Weight to Uniformly Traverse the Pareto Front
Preliminary slides Code

SURF studies how scalarization weights control the traversal speed along the Pareto front by analyzing the Pareto front geometry. Building on this, SURF proposes a geometry-guided weight sampling rule for uniform Pareto-front coverage. We apply the method to multi-objective RL and multi-objective LLM alignment.
